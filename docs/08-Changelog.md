- 2026-08-21 — **FatSecret research closed out; restaurant provider DEFERRED to pre-launch selection.** Founder decision after the Phase 1 checkpoint: VITA's architecture wins. FatSecret Basic is genuinely free (self-signup, no card, 5,000 calls/day, US dataset) and its restaurant coverage looks useful, but its Developer Terms require removing or re-requesting any Content not explicitly *storable indefinitely* within 24 hours — and that list is identifiers only, excluding nutrition values, names, brands, servings, and images. VITA stores all of those permanently in `FoodEntry` on purpose, which is what makes offline history, immediate Fuel/Home rendering, and historical consistency possible. A second blocker: FatSecret binds OAuth 2.0 tokens to an IP allowlist and Supabase Edge Functions cannot provide static egress IPs. **Restaurant coverage is not delivered and is not marked complete.** No adapter, Edge Function, credentials, attribution UI, or persistence change was added, and no compromise workaround was built — `fatsecret` stays excluded from every persisted-definition allowlist. Recorded a launch-readiness follow-up for restaurant provider selection, with the eight unresolved FatSecret questions preserved verbatim. **Documentation only — no application code changed.**

- 2026-08-21 — **Fuel final visual polish + barcode recovery.** The Fuel redesign is founder-approved and **locked**; structure untouched. (1) **Contextual food visuals were confidently wrong** — the generic fallback was itself a specific food (Ionicons' one general food glyph is a *burger and a drink*, shared with five other categories), and `banana` pointed at an apple. Replaced with **14 hand-drawn VITA illustrations** in `foodArt.ts` rendered through the existing `react-native-svg` dependency — banana, apple, egg, burger, pizza, taco, burrito, chips, bottle, coffee, drumstick, bread, bowl, and a fork-and-knife generic that cannot be mistaken for a dish. Categories with no honest drawing resolve to the generic rather than borrowing another food's picture. Classifier gained plural tolerance and a small explicit branded-term list (Big Mac → burger, Cheetos → chips), all 40 founder-named cases verified against the compiled module. (2) **Calorie ring** — the number and `Calories` are back inside the circle, and **over-target is stated rather than hidden**: `326 · Calories over` replaces a flat `0 remaining`, in amber rather than red, with ring and bar capped at 100% while the percentage keeps counting (`116% of 2,000 Calories`). New `over()`/`caloriesOver` in the nutrition domain. (3) **Barcode recovery** — a subtle `Not the right product?` on scanner-originated Food Detail only (origin tracked by route parameter, never inferred from the provider) opening a native sheet: Search for food · Scan again · Add manually · Report incorrect product, plus a `Source: Open Food Facts` provenance line. **Reporting has no backend and says so** — nothing is transmitted, and the dialog shows the barcode and identity to pass on by hand; submission is recorded as a deferred capability. Exact GTIN lookup unchanged: correct record or honest not-found, with no Kroger special case. The temporary on-screen barcode trace panel is removed; console diagnostics remain.

- 2026-08-21 — **Fuel polish + barcode QA.** Founder approved the `dee1ddf` Fuel redesign; its layout is now locked. Four follow-ups: (1) **Copy** — `cal eaten` → `Calories consumed` beside `Calories remaining`, and macros read as progress toward the user's configured targets (`Protein Goal · 30 / 160 g`), with no warning state at 100% and no invented dietary rules. (2) **Food images were being destroyed by persistence** — `parseCustomFoodShape()` rebuilt stored foods field by field and never copied `imageUrl`, and because `toggleFavorite` rewrites the already-parsed array, the next toggle erased the image from storage permanently; the stripped favorite then shadowed the intact cached copy in `findFood()`. Fixed at the source: the read path preserves `imageUrl`/`restaurant`/`dataQuality`, and `FoodEntry` gained an `imageUrl` snapshot so a logged food renders from its own history with no lookup. `restaurant` and `dataQuality` were silently lost the same way and are also restored. (3) **Contextual food visuals** — a shared three-tier resolver (real provider image → VITA category visual → generic) with 24 categories and conservative order-sensitive matching, used by Fuel's meals, Search, Recents, Favorites, Food Detail, and Edit Entry through one `FoodAvatar`. **Architecture only — custom VITA artwork is NOT done**; categories currently share stock glyphs and are the thing artwork will replace. (4) **Kroger barcode — still open.** The previous round's "Open Food Facts data is not mislabelled" conclusion was wrong: OFF record `0011110816405` sits under Kroger's GS1 company prefix `0011110` but carries Hillshire Farm "BEEF SMOKED SAUSAGE" name, brand, imagery, and nutrition. Upstream data corruption, which every VITA-side identity check correctly confirms because the code returned *is* the code requested. Not fixed and not worked around — nothing hardcoded, special-cased, or text-matched. The trace now covers camera → normalization → provider identity → navigation → Food Detail → **log snapshot** → Edit Entry, and renders on both Food Detail and Edit Entry; one physical scan will confirm the code.

- 2026-08-19 — **USDA live verification: reliability + ranking fixes.** Removed the `dataType` query parameter, which api.data.gov intermittently rejected with nginx `400` on **5 of 10** identical requests (unfiltered: 10/10) — it was silently costing USDA results on about half of all searches. Filtering moved client-side. Also removed a `hasCompleteMacros` ranking bonus that rewarded food type rather than data quality, pushing "Bread, egg" above real eggs.

- 2026-08-19 — **USDA ranking fix.** Real FoodData Central responses showed bare generic queries returning branded junk first — a peanut butter spread named "BANANA" above "Bananas, raw", three products named "EGG" above real eggs. Added `VitaFood.dataQuality`, a provider-independent per-record trust signal (USDA maps its FDC data types; Open Food Facts uses a flat value), reduced the exact-name bonus for branded products whose brand the query never mentioned, and added a capped extra-word penalty. All slice 2.6 field and per-100 g normalization assumptions were confirmed correct.
